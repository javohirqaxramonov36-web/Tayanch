Unit circle: sin and cos are the coordinates of a point

On a circle with a radius of 1, an angle marks a single point. The x-coordinate of that point is cos, and the y-coordinate is sin. Drag the point and watch how the values change.

After this lesson

  • You convert degrees to radians and radians to degrees.
  • You find the sin and cos values of 30°, 45°, 60° and their related angles.
  • You determine the sign from the quadrant and apply the rule sin x = cos(90° − x).

Exercises

Sample questions written for Tayanch, not official SAT questions. No score is calculated.

Exercise 1

SampleeasyDegrees and radiansnot verified

What is 150° in radians?

Solution. Radians = degrees · π/180. 150 · π/180 = 5π/6. Check: 5π/6 · 180/π = 150.

Exercise 2

SampleeasySpecial anglesnot verified

What is sin 30°?

Solution. At 30°, the point on the unit circle is (√3/2, 1/2). sin is the y-coordinate: 1/2.

Exercise 3

SamplemediumQuadrant and signnot verified

What is cos(2π/3)?

Solution. 2π/3 = 120°. The point is (−1/2, √3/2), and cos is the x-coordinate: −1/2. cos is negative in quadrant II.

Exercise 4

SamplemediumComplementary anglesnot verified

If 0° < x < 90° and sin x = cos 20°, what is x?

Solution. sin x = cos(90° − x). So 90 − x = 20, and x = 70. Check: sin 70° ≈ 0.940 and cos 20° ≈ 0.940.

Exercise 5

SamplehardAngle from a pointnot verified

Point P on the unit circle is (−√3/2, 1/2). What angle does ray OP make with the positive x-axis (from 0° to 360°)?

Solution. cos = −√3/2 < 0 and sin = 1/2 > 0: quadrant II. The reference angle is 30°, so in quadrant II the angle is 180° − 30° = 150°.

Summary

  • The point on the unit circle is (cos θ, sin θ). tan θ = sin θ / cos θ, undefined when cos θ = 0.
  • Radians = degrees · π/180. 180° = π, 90° = π/2.
  • In quadrant I all are positive, in II only sin, in III only tan, in IV only cos.
  • sin x = cos(90° − x): complementary angles swap their sin and cos values.
  • If you work in radians on a calculator, check that it is in radian mode.
