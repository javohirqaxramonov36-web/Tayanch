Volume: base area times height

Build a cylinder from thin disks and the formula appears on its own. Then change the dimensions of a cone, a sphere and a prism and watch how the volume grows.

After this lesson

  • You can explain with disks why the volume of a cylinder is πr²h.
  • You calculate the volume of a cone, a sphere and a prism with the right formula.
  • You do not confuse the radius with the diameter, or r² with r³.

We build the cylinder from identical disks with a thickness of 1. The volume of each disk is base area × thickness = πr² × 1. The volume is πr² times the number of disks.

Exercises

Sample questions written for Tayanch, not official SAT questions. No score is calculated.

Exercise 1

SampleeasyVolume of a cylindernot verified

A cylinder has a radius of 3 and a height of 5. What is the volume of the cylinder?

Solution. V = πr²h = π · 3² · 5 = π · 9 · 5 = 45π.

Exercise 2

SamplemediumVolume of a conenot verified

A cone has a base radius of 6 and a height of 4. What is the volume of the cone?

Solution. V = ⅓πr²h = ⅓ · π · 36 · 4 = ⅓ · 144π = 48π.

Exercise 3

SamplemediumVolume of a spherenot verified

A sphere has a diameter of 6. What is the volume of the sphere?

Solution. The radius is half the diameter: r = 3. V = ⁴⁄₃πr³ = ⁴⁄₃ · π · 27 = 36π.

Exercise 4

SampleeasyRectangular prismnot verified

A rectangular prism has a length of 5, a width of 4 and a volume of 120. What is its height?

Solution. V = l · w · h, so 120 = 5 · 4 · h = 20h. h = 120 ÷ 20 = 6. Check: 5 · 4 · 6 = 120.

Exercise 5

SamplehardChanging dimensionsnot verified

The radius of a cylinder is doubled, and its height is halved. How does its volume change?

Solution. New volume: π(2r)² · (h/2) = π · 4r² · h/2 = 2πr²h. This is 2 times the old volume.

Exercise 6

SamplehardCylinder and conenot verified

A cylindrical container with a radius of 3 cm and a height of 10 cm is full of water. The water is poured into cone-shaped containers with a radius of 3 cm and a height of 10 cm. How many cones will be filled?

Solution. Cylinder: π · 9 · 10 = 90π cm³. Cone: ⅓ · π · 9 · 10 = 30π cm³. 90π ÷ 30π = 3.

Summary

  • Cylinder and prism: V = base area × height. For a cylinder, the base area is πr².
  • A cone is one third of the cylinder with the same base and height: V = ⅓πr²h.
  • A sphere depends only on the radius: V = ⁴⁄₃πr³. If the radius doubles, the volume becomes 8 times as large.
  • If a question gives the diameter, divide it by 2 first.
