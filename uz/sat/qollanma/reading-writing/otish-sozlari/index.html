Transition words: first the relationship, then the word

In a transition question, all the options are grammatically correct. The difference is in meaning: the word states the relationship between the two sentences. First decide what sentence 2 does to sentence 1, then choose a word for that relationship.

After this lesson

  • You sort the relationship between two sentences into one of four types.
  • You know the transition words for each relationship and do not mix them up.
  • You do not fall for the trap of another connecting word inside the sentence (for example, "so").

Sample Example 1 of 4

The museum's new wing was designed to hold large sculptures. ______ most of the pieces shown there in its first year were small paintings.

The blank in the scene is filled in, and the table marks the relationship for this pair with a blue border. Use "Next example" to see each of the four relationships.

Why this relationshipThe wing was built for large sculptures, but small paintings were shown in it: the opposite of what was expected.
Contrast However · In contrast · Nevertheless
Does sentence 2 go against sentence 1 or say something unexpected?
Cause and effect Therefore · As a result · Consequently
Does sentence 2 follow from sentence 1?
Example For example · For instance · Specifically
Is sentence 2 a specific case of the general idea in sentence 1?
Addition Moreover · In addition · Furthermore
Does sentence 2 add a new idea in the same direction?

Method: cover the blank, connect the two sentences in your own words ("but", "so", "for example", "also"), then find the option with that meaning.

Task. Read the two sentences in the scene and choose their relationship from the table. With a keyboard: press Tab to enter the table, move with the arrow keys, press Enter to choose.

Exercises

Sample questions written for Tayanch, not official SAT questions. No score is calculated.

Exercise 1

SampleeasyContrast: a common belief and a factnot verified

Choose the most logical transition word for the blank.

Many people assume that cacti need almost no water. ______ most cactus species grow faster and flower more often when they are watered regularly during the warm months.

Solution. Sentence 1 is what many people assume (cacti need almost no water); sentence 2 is a fact that goes against it (they grow better when watered regularly). Relationship: contrast, so "However,".

Exercise 2

SampleeasyCause and effect: an event and its consequencenot verified

Choose the most logical transition word for the blank.

The village's only road was buried under a landslide in early spring. ______ the weekly market was moved to a field on the other side of the river until the repairs were finished.

Solution. The buried road is the cause; moving the market to another place is its result. Relationship: cause and effect, so "Therefore,".

Exercise 3

SamplemediumExample: a general idea and a specific casenot verified

Choose the most logical transition word for the blank.

Some birds use tools to reach food they could not get otherwise. ______ certain crows bend thin twigs into hooks and use them to pull insects out of narrow holes in tree bark.

Solution. Sentence 1 is a general idea (some birds use tools); sentence 2 is a specific case of it (crows make hooks from twigs). It is not a new idea but an example of the same idea. So "For instance,".

Exercise 4

SamplemediumAddition: a second benefit in the same directionnot verified

Choose the most logical transition word for the blank.

Planting trees along the school's parking lot kept the parked cars noticeably cooler in summer. ______ the trees' roots helped hold the soil in place during heavy rain, so less mud washed onto the nearby path.

Solution. Both sentences are about the benefits of the trees: one is shade, the other is holding the soil. The roots holding the soil is not a result of the cars staying cool. Relationship: addition, so "In addition,".

Exercise 5

SamplehardContrast: in spite of the drawbacksnot verified

Choose the most logical transition word for the blank.

The team's first prototype of the water filter was heavy, slow, and expensive to build. ______ it removed nearly all of the sediment from the river water during the field test, so the engineers decided to keep its basic design.

Solution. Sentence 1 lists the drawbacks; sentence 2 states an achievement in spite of them. Relationship: contrast (concession), so "Nevertheless,". The "so" in sentence 2 is a different connection: it links the achievement and the decision.

Summary

  • Cover the blank and ask: what does sentence 2 do to sentence 1?
  • If it goes against it: However, In contrast, Nevertheless. If it follows from it: Therefore, As a result, Consequently.
  • If it is a specific case: For example, For instance. If it adds a new idea: Moreover, In addition, Furthermore.
  • Words for the same relationship can often replace each other; the answer options, though, usually have one word from each relationship.
  • A second benefit is not a result of the first: use "As a result" only when there is a cause.
