Mark the evidence: which sentence proves the claim

In an evidence question, every sentence seems close to the topic. The difference: evidence is an observed fact, and it is about exactly the factor in the claim. Tap a sentence in the text, then see why it is or is not evidence.

After this lesson

  • You spot the main factor of a claim (what, who, what change) at a glance.
  • You tell context, method and a sentence that restates the conclusion apart from evidence.
  • You do not fall for the trap of a sentence that is close to the topic but unrelated to the claim.

Lab

Sample Made-up situation

Claim A layer of straw helps soil stay moist.

Each sentence in the scene has a label that shows its role. The evidence sentence has a blue border and an underline; the others have a dashed border.

Why this sentenceAn observed result about exactly the factor in the claim (moisture): this is the evidence.
Evidence
An observed fact or result, and it is directly about the factor in the claim.
Context, question, method
Tells the situation or what was done, not the result.
Restated claim
Says the conclusion in other words. It is not proof; it is what needs to be proved.
Close to the topic
On the same topic, but not about the factor in the claim.
Opposing view
A view the author rejects. It goes against the claim.

Task. Read the claim and tap the sentence in the scene's text that supports it. With a keyboard: press Tab to enter the text, move with the arrow keys, press Enter to mark.

Exercises

Sample questions written for Tayanch, not official SAT questions. No score is calculated.

Exercise 1

SampleeasyScience: an experiment resultnot verified

The students in the class concluded that light, not water, caused the difference in how the plants grew. Which sentence shows that water was not the cause?

Text: tap the sentence that serves as evidence

Solution. The claim has two parts: light caused the difference, and water was not the cause. If both groups received the same amount of water, water cannot explain the difference. Only sentence 2 rules out water.

Exercise 2

SamplemediumSocial science: an observationnot verified

The library staff believe that the evening hours especially benefited adults who work during the day. Which sentence most directly supports this idea?

Text: tap the sentence that serves as evidence

Solution. The claim is about who benefited from the evening hours. That most of the late visitors were adults who came straight from work shows exactly this.

Exercise 3

SamplemediumLiterature: characternot verified

The text portrays the clockmaker as a person who puts helping others ahead of his own work. Which sentence best supports this?

Text: tap the sentence that serves as evidence

Solution. The claim has two parts: his own work and helping others. In sentence 3, the clockmaker sets his own work aside, helps the boy and takes no payment, so both parts are there.

Exercise 4

SamplemediumCulture: handicraftnot verified

The family believes that handmade paper lasts for many years without falling apart. Which sentence best supports this claim?

Text: tap the sentence that serves as evidence

Solution. The claim is that the paper does not fall apart over time. The pages of a notebook made long ago are still intact, and this shows it directly.

Exercise 5

SamplehardScience: conclusion and evidencenot verified

A student concluded that pigeons do not find their way home only by seeing familiar buildings. Which sentence best supports this conclusion?

Text: tap the sentence that serves as evidence

Solution. Evidence must be an observed result. The birds returned even from a place with no familiar landmarks, and this supports the conclusion. Sentence 4 is the conclusion itself, so it cannot count as evidence.

Summary

  • First read the claim and pick out its factor: what exactly needs to be proved.
  • Evidence is an observed fact or result. Question, method and context sentences are not evidence.
  • A sentence that restates the claim in other words is not evidence: it is the conclusion itself.
  • A sentence close to the topic is a trap: if it is not about the factor in the claim, it is not evidence.
