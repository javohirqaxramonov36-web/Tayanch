Where to start with the SAT®

Studying on your own and not sure what comes first? Answer four questions: you get a step-by-step list you can tick off. Below it is a map of free resources, official ones first.

No score is guaranteed. The steps and their order are a Tayanch recommendation, not an official College Board plan. Your answers and check marks stay only in this browser.

My path

1Do you have a test date?

Enter the date yourself. The official list of dates is on the College Board website.

2Where are you now?
3How many hours a week can you study?
4Budget

You can skip any question: the list below updates as you answer.

Your path

0 of 7 steps done

  1. In the Bluebook app, open Practice and Prepare and take a full-length practice test. Do not study for it first: the score is only your starting point.

    Take it in one sitting, like test day, somewhere quiet.

    • Download Bluebook™ (opens in a new tab)
    • How to use Bluebook practice tests (opens in a new tab)

  2. In My Practice, open Score Details: you see every question, your answer and the correct answer.

    Write down which skills you missed and how many. This list decides what you practice next.

    • Tayanch test tracker
    • My Practice (opens in a new tab)

  3. Turn your missed skills into a weekly plan with the Study planner.

    • Study planner

  4. Practice the skills from step 2. Filter the Student Question Bank or the Tayanch question bank by skill.

    On Khan Academy, each skill has three levels: start at Foundations, then Medium, then Advanced.

    • Tayanch question bank
    • Student Question Bank (opens in a new tab)
    • Official SAT Prep on Khan Academy® (opens in a new tab)

  5. Every few weeks, take another full-length Bluebook test to check your progress under time. Use short timed sets between them.

    • Tayanch short tests
    • Full-length tests in Bluebook (opens in a new tab)

  6. For each mistake, note why: you did not know the skill, misread the question, or ran out of time.

    Come back to the same questions after a few days. After each Bluebook test, Khan Academy has a walk-through of every question.

    • Mistake log: review
    • Khan Academy practice test breakdowns (opens in a new tab)

  7. After your test, compare your score with what your universities ask for and with their deadlines. Rules and dates for the next test are on the official SAT® website.

    • Timing and retakes guide: coming soon
    • The SAT® on College Board (opens in a new tab)
This clears your answers and check marks.

VERIFIED11.11.2026

Why start with a full practice test?

A College Board research report (May 2025, SAT takers in the high school class of 2025) found that students who completed 1, 2, and 3 or more full-length practice tests in Bluebook scored approximately 25, 45 and 60 points higher than similar students who completed none.

Correlation, not cause. The report itself says the estimates are not causal: students who practice more may differ in other ways. This is not a promise for your score.

Source: College Board Research, May 2025 (PDF). Checked 2026-11-11.
